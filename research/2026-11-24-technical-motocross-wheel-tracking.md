# Wheel tracking in MotoSim

November 24, 2026. This is a source and code review of sideways drift and abrupt turning. The cause of the reported episode needs a recorded reproduction.

## Simulator references

PiBoSo documents an automotive-industry tire model, chassis flex, dynamic terrain deformation, and wet terrain. Its changelog also identifies virtual-rider fixes. Its output plugin is a route to measuring observable behavior in repeatable runs. The reviewed documentation leaves the current tire equations, coefficients, and controller gains unspecified. [MX Bikes overview](https://www.mx-bikes.com/?page=about) · [Changelog](https://www.mx-bikes.com/?page=news) · [Output plugin](https://forum.mx-bikes.com/index.php?topic=96.0)

MX Simulator developer Josh Vanderhoof documents friction curves indexed by slip speed for each track tile, separate rolling resistance, and erosion that can respond to lateral motion and front-wheel force. He also documents adjustable steering strength, damping and torque limits, with a blend between requesting lean and applying bar torque. That makes MX Simulator a concrete reference for separating surface tuning from rider-control tuning. [Track tile specification](https://forum.mxsimulator.com/viewtopic.php?t=3166) · [Steering and stability controls](https://forum.mxsimulator.com/viewtopic.php?t=4802)

Milestone's MXGP manual documents three physics levels. It provides a reference for exposing different levels of assistance. The manual gives no tire equations to calibrate against. [MXGP manual](https://cdn.cloudflare.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/256370/manuals/MX2013_Manual_ENG.pdf?t=1576506027)

## What the current solver suggests

MotoSim already computes per-wheel slip from contact velocity, applies load-limited combined slip and camber thrust, and lets the front contact force turn the fork about its steering axis. At 30 degrees of contact camber and zero slip, the current curve alone requests about 62 percent of peak grip laterally once the low-speed blend is fully active. This warrants a calibration sweep across both wheels. It does not establish that camber caused the reported slide. [Motorcycle.swift](../MotoSim/Physics/Motorcycle.swift#L327) · [DirtTire.swift](../MotoSim/Physics/DirtTire.swift#L11)

The solver calculates a rounded, lean-dependent contact offset, but samples the height, normal and material at the axle's horizontal coordinates. A leaned tire near a berm edge or surface boundary can therefore take its grip and support direction from the wrong part of the track. On identical terrain, both wheels use the same force curve and reach its current target immediately at each physics step. The contact forces and steering servo can both change yaw, so changes to peak grip alone could mask the source. [Motorcycle.swift](../MotoSim/Physics/Motorcycle.swift#L337) · [Motorcycle.swift steering integration](../MotoSim/Physics/Motorcycle.swift#L436)

## Implementation order

1. **Capture a baseline.** Replay a straight coast with a small yaw or lateral disturbance, a steady lean turn through coast, brake and drive, and a leaned berm crossing or landing. Extend TireTrace to record signed longitudinal and lateral slip velocities, solver-effective slip, camber, normal load, separate force components, front mechanical-trail torque and rider torque, and yaw rate. Record tire inputs from the same physics instant. The existing HUD slip ratio combines pre-step patch speed with post-step wheel speed and uses a different denominator from the force calculation. [TireTrace.swift](../MotoSim/Diagnostics/TireTrace.swift#L15) · [Motorcycle.swift wheel-speed update](../MotoSim/Physics/Motorcycle.swift#L420)
2. **Use the failure signature to choose the first physics change.** If the kick coincides with a change in normal or material on a berm or rut edge, solve contact and sample ground consistently at the rounded patch. If the bike walks sideways on smooth ground at low speed while grip is available, prototype bounded static sticking or brush compliance, with both tires sharing the chassis response if coupling matters. If the fork and yaw overshoot on smooth ground while rolling, check rider torque versus contact torque and compare the existing tire law with a per-wheel relaxation model before retuning assist. [Lot's experimental motorcycle tire model](https://doi.org/10.1023/B%3AMECC.0000022842.12077.5C) · [Measured motorcycle tire transient response](https://www.research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3137326?mode=complete)
3. **Tune tracking and breakaway independently.** Fit front and rear small-slip lateral stiffness, camber thrust and post-peak response. Small-slip stiffness sets how much the tire has to drift to generate a given cornering force. Peak and post-peak grip govern breakaway and recovery. A rolling-distance relaxation state can smooth force buildup, but excessive lag can worsen oscillation. Keep force bounded by current contact load and handle loss of contact so a landing does not inherit stale force. Sweep the resulting response through throttle and braking at lean. The road-tire studies establish mechanisms and do not provide dirt-bike calibration values. [Tire measurements](https://www.research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3137326?mode=complete)
4. **Build rut guidance from geometry.** Once basic tracking is stable, use surface-specific force curves and sample the rounded footprint across a shared rut and its shoulders. Both wheels can then respond to the same rut, and either can climb out. The rear should be able to trace a naturally tighter arc through a steady turn. [MX Simulator terrain model parameters](https://forum.mxsimulator.com/viewtopic.php?t=3166) · [MotoSim tire–soil research](2026-11-23-technical-dirt-bike-traction.md)

Judge the change by decay of lateral speed and yaw oscillation after a disturbance, repeatable curvature for a held lean, progressive breakaway and recovery, and a clean transition back to grip after touchdown. Compare wheel paths, signed slip and peak yaw rate against the baseline. MX Bikes and MX Simulator can serve as ride-feel comparisons on comparable surfaces and at comparable speed, with the assist settings recorded. No side-by-side rides or incident telemetry were available for this review.
