# Motorcycle steering and balance in MotoSim

This November 23, 2026 review covers virtual-rider steering, steering-head geometry, and contact-patch kinematics. It investigates why MotoSim’s front and rear tires could feel disconnected in a turn. The implementation uses published techniques in the existing 240 Hz solver.

## What the simulators document

MX Simulator’s developer describes Direct Steering as a blend between a target lean handled by a steering controller and direct player force at the bars. The developer also documents independent steering strength, damping, force limit, and low-speed roll damping. PiBoSo’s MX Bikes release notes confirm direct-lean controls, virtual-rider simulation, fork and rear-linkage simulation, chassis flex, and deformable terrain. I did not find a published control law or complete force model for MX Bikes. The strongest comparison is the publicly documented division between rider intent, physical steering, and chassis response. [MX Simulator developer explanation](https://forum.mxsimulator.com/viewtopic.php?f=1&start=15&t=1085) · [PiBoSo MX Bikes release notes](https://www.mx-bikes.com/?page=news)

Motorcycle dynamics research models steering torque as a control input to achieve a requested lean. In a multibody model, steering-head geometry and the front-tire contact forces influence the torque at the bars. The tire contact frame lies in the wheel and road planes. The velocity at the contact is needed for both along-wheel and sideways slip. Experimental motorcycle data support both side-slip and camber contributions. These studies provide mechanisms and validation methods. Their road-tire coefficients are not motocross calibration data. [Sharp, Evangelou, and Limebeer, motorcycle dynamics](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/fa580662-2e51-4a70-a9b9-d0144097b67b) · [Cossalter and Lot, contact kinematics](https://www.research.unipd.it/retrieve/e14fb267-9ff3-3de1-e053-1705fe0ac030/1999_01%20Motorcycle%20dynamic%20simulation%20TYRE%20TECHNOLOGY.pdf) · [Honda motorcycle tire experiments](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/hondatechnicalreview/12/1/12_2000_12_1_21/_supplement/_download/12_2000_12_1_21_1.pdf)

## What changed in MotoSim

The previous solver assigned a smoothed steering angle and applied a strong roll assist at any grounded speed. Its camber contribution acted outward when the bike leaned. It also used velocity at the wheel hub for longitudinal slip and velocity at the contact patch for lateral slip. These are concrete inconsistencies that remain even when the grip slider is increased. [Motorcycle implementation](../MotoSim/Physics/Motorcycle.swift) · [Lean command](../MotoSim/Physics/LeanSteering.swift)

MotoSim now gives the front assembly steering inertia. The virtual rider commands bounded handlebar torque based on the lean error, roll rate, and steering angle and rate. At riding speed, the controller briefly countersteers to initiate lean and accounts for the lean already achieved when computing steering into the turn. The front axle and wheel rotate about a raked steering axis. The front contact lever, including trail, gives the road reaction a moment on that axis. The handlebars and wheel are rendered from the same steering state. Direct roll assistance tapers at speed and remains active at low speeds. [Virtual rider controller study](https://doi.org/10.1177/0954407012440217) · [MotoSim steering physics](../MotoSim/Physics/Motorcycle.swift) · [Fork and bar rendering](../MotoSim/Rendering/BikeRenderer.swift)

The rolling direction is now perpendicular to both the wheel axle and the road normal. Longitudinal and lateral slip share one contact-patch velocity, including motion from steering rate. Camber thrust now points toward the lean in symmetric straight-rolling tire tests. Chassis yaw still comes from contact forces. ## Validation and limits

Regression tests check the sign of camber thrust under mirrored lean, rolling direction on a slope, and the transient from opposite bar angle into a completed turn. The existing acceleration, braking, wheelie, jump and landing tests also exercise the revised contact kinematics. The controller gains, trail, and steering inertia are provisional. The model retains direct roll assistance and does not explicitly model frame flex, tire carcass relaxation, or soil displacement. The separate [soil traction report](2026-11-23-technical-dirt-bike-traction.md) covers those terrain effects. A same-corner iPhone comparison using the tire overlays is needed to judge handling feel.
