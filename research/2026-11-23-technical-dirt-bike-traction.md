# Dirt-bike tire traction for MotoSim

Research date is November 23, 2026. Scope covers a motocross front and rear tire on hardpack, loam, loose gravel, sand, and mud. This report evaluates models for MotoSim’s 240 Hz Swift solver. It uses experimental tire and soil studies, published models, and inspection of the current code. Results from rover, tractor, and automotive tires inform the mechanisms and model structure. Tire-specific coefficients require motocross measurements.

## Finding

Use a hybrid tire model. Keep a transient brush model for rigid ground. On deformable dirt, add a small patch model that calculates soil pressure, accumulated shear, and resistance from embedded knobs and sidewalls. Give each wheel its own patch and contact history. Once those forces are validated, add persistent ruts so both tires can respond to the same physical line. A grip multiplier for sand or a force that pulls the rear toward the front wheel’s path would miss the mechanism. [Project Chrono SCM](https://api.projectchrono.org/development/vehicle_terrain.html) · [Ishigami et al., steering experiments on loose soil](https://k-nagatani.org/pdf/2007-JFR-Genya-online.pdf) · [Brush–soil fusion study, publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0020740325012068)

## What MotoSim calculates today

[Track.swift](../MotoSim/Terrain/Track.swift) sets grip to 1.08 in the lane and blends toward 0.63 away from it. [DirtTire.swift](../MotoSim/Physics/DirtTire.swift) converts longitudinal slip and lateral slip angle plus camber into a combined demand vector. Its peak is approximately normal load multiplied by the local grip coefficient and an empirical load correction. It retains 74% to 88% of peak at large combined slip, depending on the dominant direction. With an 850 N load at lane center the peak is about 918 N. [Motorcycle.swift](../MotoSim/Physics/Motorcycle.swift) estimates support from suspension compression and evaluates force around one nominal contact per wheel. The solver cannot tell whether the tire is resting on hardpack, digging into sand, or pushing against the side of a rut. The balance assist also applies roll torque, and steering commands a fork angle without a steering degree of freedom for trail or self-aligning torque. Log these separately when diagnosing a steering or skid complaint. The current slip denominators include timestep-dependent stabilization. Fit soil behavior against raw patch velocity and accumulated displacement, and record solver-effective slip separately.

## The equations to use

1. **Normal support and sinkage.** The Bekker pressure–sinkage law is `p = (kc/b + kφ) z^n`. Here `p` is local normal pressure in Pa, `z` is soil sinkage in meters, `b` is the characteristic footprint width in meters, and `kc`, `kφ`, and `n` are fitted soil parameters. Sum `p × area` across the patch for normal force. For sloped or curved contact, resolve each contribution along its contact normal. For a wheel with combined surface overlap, solve for suspension compression and ground sinkage separately so the same ground reaction supports both. Comparing suspension sag directly to soil sinkage double-counts deformation. Fit the soil law using plate tests at multiple widths. [Project Chrono contact model and footprint approximation](https://api.projectchrono.org/development/vehicle_terrain.html) · [Gallina et al., soil parameter identification](https://elib.dlr.de/104615/1/jrf_paper.pdf)
2. **Under-tread shear.** Mohr–Coulomb gives a local shear limit `τmax = c + p tan(φ)`. Janosi–Hanamoto describes how it develops with relative displacement `j`: `τ = τmax [1 − exp(−|j|/K)]`. Here `c` is cohesion in Pa, `φ` is soil internal friction angle, and `K` is a characteristic shear displacement in meters. Advect a two-dimensional displacement along the footprint as new tread enters and leaves. If the vector measures tread motion relative to soil, the local force points opposite that vector. It is zero at zero displacement. Integrate stress times area to obtain both force components. Use a bounded brush deflection or supported sticking solve for near-zero speed, including a locked wheel. The classical shear law saturates monotonically. Apply a calibrated fall from peak to residual only on soils where measurements show it. [Chrono technical report](https://sbel.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/569/2018/05/TR-2017-01.pdf) · [Deformable tire–soil parameter identification](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022489825000436)
3. **Knob and sidewall resistance.** Approximate the exposed forward faces of center knobs, shoulder knobs, and the buried side of the tire separately. An optional passive-pressure starting model for one immersed rectangular face is `Kp = tan²(45° + φ/2)`, `F ≈ w[½ ρg Kp h² + 2c √Kp h]`, where `w` is face width and `h` is engaged depth. This is the zero-surcharge flat-blade approximation. For a leaned tire, use directional projected areas and measured or bounded effective face widths. Act on faces moving into the soil and apply the opposite reaction at the local point of action. Calibrate against a baseline tread so adjacent knobs do not each claim the same volume of soil or duplicate thrust already represented by the shear model. The formula comes from grouser models and its constants require motocross validation. [Politecnico di Torino passive-pressure grouser model](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/handle/11583/2847761/e384c432-4427-d4b2-e053-9f05fe0a1d67/Grousers%20Effect%20in%20Tracked%20Vehicle%20Multibody%20Dynamics%20with%20Deformable%20Terrain%20Contact%20Model.pdf) · [Ishigami et al., lateral shear and bulldozing](https://k-nagatani.org/pdf/2007-JFR-Genya-online.pdf)

Forward net force is tread and knob thrust minus rolling and compaction resistance. A buried sidewall can oppose sideslip as the sand in front of the tire consumes energy. Excessive wheelspin can also excavate soil and reduce the support the wheel pushes against. Laboratory dry-sand experiments found separate dependence on sinkage, slip ratio, and sideslip angle. That small-tire study controlled sinkage and used level Toyoura sand, so its curves do not supply motocross parameters. The dry-sand study found the force magnitude at a given sinkage could be approximated by one curve across sideslip angles. The prepared clay study found different longitudinal and lateral limits. Both are reasons to fit each surface independently. If the pressure integration already produces longitudinal compaction drag through an asymmetric pressure distribution, a separate drag term should cover only unmodeled losses. [Combined-slip clay experiment](https://auetd.auburn.edu/handle/10415/4630) · [Ishigami et al., Eq. 16](https://k-nagatani.org/pdf/2007-JFR-Genya-online.pdf) [Dry-sand tire experiments](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022489814000640) · [Agarwal et al., dynamic granular intrusion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8064642/) · [Senatore and Sandu, off-road tire model](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022489811000401)

## Surface presets and their calibration

| Surface | Starting model and what to measure |
| --- | --- |
| Dry hardpack | Use a high-support brush with little bulk sinkage. Fit independent front and rear small-slip stiffness, peak and residual force, and drive versus braking response. Treat dusty hardpack as a loose layer over a firm base. |
| Tilled or moist loam | Fit pressure and shear together. Fit the penetration and effectiveness of center and shoulder knobs, and measure whether a second pass compacts it or shears it loose at the tested moisture. |
| Loose gravel | Model the loose layer over the base separately. Fit from wheel tests because stone size and layer depth relative to the knobs can change whether the tread reaches the substrate. |
| Dry sand | Use near-zero cohesion as an initial assumption for clean dry sand. Fit sinkage, combined slip, compaction drag, and lateral wedge resistance. Add slip-driven excavation when the baseline model reproduces low-slip cases. |
| Wet clay and mud | Split moist clay from saturated mud. Fit pressure, shear, and drag at the tested moisture. A tread-fill and clearing state is a candidate for saturated, sticky conditions, pending measurements. |

These are model choices and calibration priorities. Published granular tests compare gravel and sand under controlled preparation. Clay and loam studies use different loads and preparation methods. The Bridgestone sand-tire and wet-soil grouser results support a qualitative role for tread geometry, not transferable force coefficients. [Gravel and sand soil-bin study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022489822000581) · [Combined slip on deformable clay](https://auetd.auburn.edu/handle/10415/4630) · [Wet-soil grouser experiments](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0472/12/10/1581) · [Bridgestone X10 design](https://tires.bridgestone.com/en-us/tires/motorcycle/battlecross/x10)

Front and rear should have their own width, profile, inflation pressure, and center and shoulder tread parameters. Lean changes which knobs carry load and where the force acts. Motorcycle rotating-disc tests directly show the footprint moving and changing with camber and pressure. Controlled sand-bed tests on an automotive-sized tire found lug and block patterns raised net pull over smooth tread in part of the tested slip range. That establishes a tread effect under those test conditions. It leaves the effect size for a leaned motocross tire open. [Cossalter and Doria’s motorcycle footprint tests](https://www.research.unipd.it/handle/11577/2431262?mode=complete) · [Shinone et al., sand-bed tread comparison](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/eaef/3/2/3_2_61/_pdf)

## Why both wheels can follow a rut

A rut carries a depressed center, sloped shoulders, and soil altered by previous passes. If the rear reaches the same rut, those surfaces generate their own local reactions under its footprint. A wall can resist departure or catch and deflect the front tire when crossed. On a turn the rear can track inside the front line, and high slip or weak walls can let either tire leave the rut. Implement this by sampling footprint center and shoulders against shared terrain geometry and compaction state. An ATV model checked against field motion found the rear patch could straddle deformed and undisturbed soil. Its individual tire forces were evaluated against a higher-fidelity simulation. Advance permanent rut depth and shoulder displacement only where the soil yields. Keep transient sinkage separate from permanent rut depth. [Field-tested ATV simulation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11044-026-10196-z) · [Chrono deformable height field](https://api.projectchrono.org/development/vehicle_terrain.html) · [Off-road tire multi-pass model](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022489811000401) · [Experimental towing, traction, and multi-pass tests](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022489821000136)

## Implementation choice and validation

A road-style brush alone is useful on hardpack and cheap to run. A small Bekker–Janosi patch with brush compliance and depth-dependent bulldozing adds the mechanisms that matter on this track. Dynamic granular resistive force theory is a promising refinement for dry sand at high slip and speed, backed by rigid-wheel data. A whole-track discrete-particle or continuum simulation adds cost and calibration demands. Its best role here is an offline comparison tool if real data are unavailable. The 240 Hz performance target still needs profiling on the target iPhone. [Chrono model comparison](https://api.projectchrono.org/vehicle_terrain_crm_api_.html) · [Dynamic granular force study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8064642/)

First add surface records and per-wheel telemetry for normal load, actual tread slip velocities, slip angle, lean, footprint, sinkage, force components, aligning moment, and rider-assist torque. Use a deterministic single-wheel fixture to sweep load, applied drive and brake torque, slip angle, and camber. Prototype a few longitudinal and lateral sample points over the rounded footprint. Confirm convergence with more samples and at 120, 240, and 480 Hz. Check that dissipative contact forces oppose slip and that surface boundaries do not generate a force spike. Then integrate both tires and add a sparse rut map. [Chrono single-wheel test rig inputs](https://api.projectchrono.org/_y_a_m_l_schema_wheel_rig_terrain.html) · [Instrumented single tire experiments](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022489814000706)

The acceptance cases are a neutral-throttle coast and straight braking test on hardpack, a constant-radius turn with separate brake and drive sweeps, sinkage and roll-down versus speed on sand, and following and crossing the same rut with both tires. Record side-slip recovery time and compare simulated wheel paths and stopping distances with controlled measurements. Wheel speed and GPS/IMU are useful for ride-level checks. To identify front and rear forces separately, use an instrumented wheel rig or an independently validated inverse-dynamics method. These sources do not provide matched full-lean motocross force curves for the user's front and rear tires across all five surfaces, so numerical presets would be provisional. Confidence is high in the mechanisms and moderate in the proposed real-time architecture. Confidence in exact tire coefficients stays low until calibration.
